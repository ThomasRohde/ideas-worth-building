# Common proposal failure modes

## Problem-statement theatre

A contributor backfills a fictional user and pain narrative around a capability they already want to use.

**Correction:** preserve the capability origin, mark the beneficiary and need as uncertain, and design an exploration that can reveal whether the capability serves anything meaningful.

## Capability in search of inevitability

A new model, API, protocol, or device is treated as proof that a product must exist.

**Correction:** distinguish “possible now” from “worth creating,” compare non-technical responses, and state what the capability uniquely enables.

## Generic AI varnish

The proposition is an existing product category plus an unspecified agent, copilot, or personalization layer.

**Correction:** identify the exact new interaction, allocation of agency, source of truth, failure boundary, or quality that changes the experience.

## Feature inventory without a point of view

The proposal accumulates plausible features but cannot explain which choices matter.

**Correction:** state the governing design principle, remove features that do not serve it, and describe excellent behaviour in a decisive scenario.

## Pitch inflation

Early uncertainty is replaced by unsupported claims about market size, transformation, disruption, or universal demand.

**Correction:** use narrower claims, attach evidence, and keep aspiration separate from prediction.

## Existing-product dismissal

A related product is found and the proposal is closed without examining the distinctive delta.

**Correction:** compare mechanisms, beneficiaries, constraints, and experience. Prior art may invalidate novelty while strengthening or redirecting the proposal.

## Duplicate blindness

Different vocabulary hides a substantially identical proposition.

**Correction:** search by underlying job, capability, desired experience, mechanism, and adjacent concepts—not title alone.

## Prototype seduction

A working demo is mistaken for evidence that the project matters or can survive real use.

**Correction:** identify what the prototype actually established, what it simulated, and which risks remain untouched.

## Research dump

An agent posts many links or summaries without connecting them to a decision.

**Correction:** explain which claim each source supports, what changed in the proposal, and what remains unresolved.

## Synthetic consensus

Multiple agent comments create the appearance of community support.

**Correction:** treat agent output as analysis, not votes. Disclose model use and seek independent human judgment.

## Score substitution

A rubric total or reaction count is presented as the promotion decision.

**Correction:** use dimensions to structure reasoning, then record the curator's qualitative judgment and trade-offs.

## Full-product experiment

The “smallest experiment” is a complete implementation because coding is cheap.

**Correction:** name the uncertainty first and choose the smallest artifact or encounter capable of reducing it.
