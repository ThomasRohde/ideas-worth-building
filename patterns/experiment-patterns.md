# Experiment patterns

Choose an experiment by the uncertainty it must reduce, not by how impressive the artifact will look.

## Experience probe

Use a storyboard, clickable path, role-play, or Wizard-of-Oz interaction to test whether the intended experience is meaningful before implementing the system behind it.

Best for: interaction quality, comprehension, trust, perceived value.

## Concierge simulation

Deliver the proposed outcome manually to a small number of participants while recording the work, judgment, and information actually required.

Best for: hidden operational complexity, beneficiary fit, service shape.

## Hardest-edge spike

Implement only the technically uncertain dependency or boundary: latency, model reliability, protocol behavior, data access, deployment constraint, or integration failure.

Best for: feasibility claims where the rest of the system is routine.

## Alternative comparison

Ask participants to complete the same meaningful task using the proposal, the current workaround, and a credible alternative. Compare outcomes and experience, not novelty preference.

Best for: proposals that may be only marginally better than existing practice.

## Repeated-use diary

Place a narrow prototype in real use over enough time for novelty to decay. Ask users to record triggers, abandonments, workarounds, trust changes, and unexpected value.

Best for: habits, recurring workflows, ambient tools, personal systems.

## Artifact test

Create the output the proposed system would produce—report, plan, model, explanation, handoff, or decision—and evaluate whether the artifact itself is valuable before automating its creation.

Best for: systems whose central claim is output quality.

## Counterfactual prototype

Prototype the proposal without its fashionable capability, or prototype a simpler non-software response. Compare what is genuinely lost.

Best for: detecting technology-led solutionism.

## Failure rehearsal

Simulate wrong, delayed, adversarial, unavailable, or ambiguous behavior. Observe whether the design fails legibly and whether recovery remains possible.

Best for: agents, automation, safety-critical or trust-sensitive systems.

## Expert boundary review

Present the mechanism and intended context to a practitioner who understands the hardest domain constraint. Ask for invalid assumptions, missing actors, and credible failure modes.

Best for: regulated, specialized, institutional, or scientific domains.
