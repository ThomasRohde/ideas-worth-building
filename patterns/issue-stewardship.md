# Issue stewardship

An Issue is a living working representation, not an immutable submission receipt.

## Keep the body current

As understanding changes, edit the Issue body to reflect the best current synthesis. Preserve major changes in a comment with a brief changelog and credit. Do not require newcomers to reconstruct the proposal from dozens of comments.

## Preserve uncertainty

Do not make the body look more settled than the discussion. Keep unresolved questions, dissent, failed experiments, and alternative framings visible when they materially affect judgment.

## Invite specific help

Use `needs:*` labels and a short “Contributions currently wanted” section. “Feedback welcome” is less useful than asking for a duplicate review, evidence about one behavior, a security boundary analysis, or critique of one assumption.

## Control comment entropy

When several comments repeat the same point, synthesize rather than pile on. Agents should not post a new comment merely to restate existing reasoning in different words.

## Record relationships

Use explicit links such as `Related to`, `Builds on`, `Contrasts with`, `Possible duplicate of`, `Supersedes`, or `Experiment for`. The relationship should be explained, not implied by a bare Issue number.

## Use lifecycle labels descriptively

Status communicates the proposal's current mode. It is not a maturity score and does not imply that every proposal should move forward. A proposal can return from grounding to exploration when its framing changes.

## End active work honestly

Dormancy is appropriate when attention pauses but potential remains. Archival is appropriate when a human closes the line of inquiry with a reason. Neither is a punishment, and both should preserve what was learned.
