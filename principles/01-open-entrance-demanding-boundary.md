# Open entrance, demanding boundary

A useful idea rarely arrives in the shape of a completed business case. It may begin as an irritation, a strange prototype, an aesthetic conviction, an unexpected capability, a half-seen pattern, or a sentence that cannot yet justify itself.

The repository therefore makes **entry cheap**:

- only the spark and the proposition are required;
- uncertainty may be explicit;
- proposals need not reference a separate problem;
- unconventional, non-commercial, playful, civic, artistic, and infrastructural ideas are welcome;
- lack of polish is not a reason for rejection.

The repository makes **promotion expensive in judgment, not bureaucracy**. Before entering `ideas/`, a proposal must demonstrate:

- who or what it serves;
- why that service matters;
- why the proposed response is apt rather than merely possible;
- what is distinctive about it;
- what excellent execution would require;
- what remains uncertain and how to learn more.

This asymmetry is intentional. A strict entrance filters out fragile originality too early. A weak promotion boundary turns curation into accumulation.
