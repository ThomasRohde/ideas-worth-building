# No problem-statement theatre

“Start with the problem” is often sound advice. It becomes harmful when treated as a ritual that every idea must perform before it can be discussed.

Some valuable projects begin elsewhere:

- a capability creates a new design space;
- a prototype produces an unexpectedly good experience;
- two mature concepts become powerful in combination;
- an observation reveals a possibility before it reveals a market;
- a craft or artistic ambition is worth pursuing without a pain-point narrative.

Contributors must not invent a persona, pain point, urgency, addressable market, or quantified demand to satisfy a template. `Unknown` is more valuable than plausible fiction.

This principle does not remove the need for grounding. It changes **when and how** grounding is demanded. A proposal can enter without a problem statement, but it cannot be promoted without explaining who or what it serves, why that matters, and why the response is appropriate.
