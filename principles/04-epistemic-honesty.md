# Epistemic honesty

A proposal becomes stronger when it shows the shape of its uncertainty.

Contributors should distinguish:

- **facts and evidence** — externally verifiable observations with sources;
- **first-hand observations** — clearly identified experience, not generalized into universal truth;
- **assumptions** — claims currently used for reasoning but not established;
- **speculation** — plausible possibilities offered for exploration;
- **preferences and judgments** — views about quality, meaning, or taste that cannot be reduced to facts.

Do not launder assumptions into evidence through confident prose. Do not turn a single anecdote into a market claim. Do not cite sources that merely mention a topic while failing to support the actual statement.

Uncertainty is not a defect to hide. The goal is to make it legible enough that others can challenge it, investigate it, or design an experiment around it.
