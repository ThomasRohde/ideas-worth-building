# Critique the strongest version

The repository values disagreement that improves decisions.

Before criticizing a proposal, state the strongest version you can reasonably infer. Then identify the most consequential weakness: the assumption that fails, the beneficiary who is misread, the alternative that dominates, the harmful incentive, the technical boundary, the quality contradiction, or the reason the experience would not matter.

Good critique is:

- specific enough to act on;
- proportionate to the evidence;
- directed at the proposal, not the contributor;
- willing to identify what would change the critic's view;
- alert to both false positives and false negatives.

“Great idea,” “this already exists,” “nobody wants this,” and “AI can build it” are not substantive contributions without supporting analysis.
