# Distinctiveness and taste

Feasibility is not enough. A project can be useful and buildable while still being generic, poorly framed, or not worth preserving as an idea.

Promotion requires an account of the proposal's **distinctive judgment**. This may be:

- a non-obvious mechanism;
- a surprising combination;
- a neglected beneficiary;
- a deliberately constrained interaction;
- a new relationship between people and a system;
- an unusual quality bar;
- a refusal of a common but damaging convention;
- an experience whose value is difficult to capture as features.

Taste is not decoration added after functionality. It is the ability to choose what belongs, what does not, what should be effortless, what should remain visible, and what kind of experience the project should create.

“Use AI,” “make it simple,” “personalize it,” and “build a platform” are not distinctive insights by themselves. They become meaningful only when attached to a precise point of view and an excellent response.
